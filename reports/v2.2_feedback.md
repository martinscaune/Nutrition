# v2.2: answers to user feedback (fibre, carbohydrate quality, vitamin C, oils, data, prices)

2026-10-07. Questions from the owner and a friend with a nutrition background. Each answer gives what the evidence
says and what changed in the tool. Source keys refer to `literature/SOURCES.md`.

## 1. Fibre

**Does cooking destroy fibre?** No. In our own data, the 20 USDA raw↔cooked pairs give a median *true retention*
of total dietary fibre of **1.06** (IQR 0.90–1.27; computed as fibre per g cooked × cooked mass ÷ fibre per g raw ×
raw mass). Per 100 g the value drops only because cooked food holds water: dry lentils have 10.7 g/100 g and boiled
lentils 7.9 g/100 g, but 100 g of dry lentils becomes 273 g cooked, so the total is unchanged or slightly higher.
Studies agree. Cooking shifts fibre between fractions:
- Processing lowered soluble and raised insoluble fibre in chickpea, cowpea and mung bean (veena1995).
- Cooking increased total dietary fibre in five legumes (njoumi2019).
- Resistant starch formed on cooking and cooling is counted as fibre.

Soluble fibre can leach into cooking water that is thrown away. The kernel of truth in the claim is that values
"per 100 g" fall after cooking. The tool already handles this: values "as eaten" = as bought ÷ cooked/raw mass ratio,
with fibre retention 1.

**Soluble vs insoluble.** Neither EFSA (efsa2010c) nor WHO sets separate targets for the two fractions. Viscous
soluble fibres (β-glucans in oats and barley, pectins) lower appetite more often than non-viscous ones, but the
effects on energy intake are small (wanders2011). The tool now tracks soluble and insoluble fibre where Frida
analysed them. That is only 15 foods; extending it needs another database (see §6).

**Targets.**

|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum | 25 g | 25 g | EFSA AI for normal laxation (efsa2010c); WHO 2023 ≥ 25 g naturally occurring fibre (who2023carb) |

Cost of the change: +2–5 % per day (owner €2.69 → €2.78). The friend's point that a sudden large increase causes
digestive trouble is common clinical advice. We found no trial that quantifies a safe rate of increase, so the tool
only says "raise intake gradually".

## 2. Fast vs slow carbohydrates (glycaemic index)
In the largest synthesis (reynolds2019: 185 prospective studies, 58 trials), fibre and whole grains predicted health
outcomes with **moderate** certainty. Glycaemic index and load did so only with **low to very low** certainty, with
smaller or no risk reductions. For training, the amount and timing of carbohydrate matter more than GI (thomas2016).
So GI is not a target. The tool now shows starch and sugars separately, and the Explorer can plot e.g.
`sugars / carb * 100`. GI values for our foods would come from the international GI tables (atkinson2021, AJCN,
paywalled), which is possible with university access.

## 3. Vitamin C
EFSA's 110 mg/day (men) and 95 mg (women) is *not* a scurvy threshold; about 10 mg/day prevents scurvy. EFSA
chose the intake that keeps fasting plasma ascorbate around 50 µmol/L, close to the point where body pools saturate
(efsa2013vitc). In depletion–repletion studies, the steep part of the plasma curve lies between 30 and 100 mg/day;
200 mg is the first dose past it, and plasma saturates at about 1,000 mg (levine1996). An RDA of 200 mg was
proposed on that basis (carr1999). For athletes, **supplements** of 1 g/day (with vitamin E) blunted endurance
training adaptations (paulsen2014, gomezcabrera2008). That concerns supplement doses, not food.

Change: an optional **"Higher vitamin C target (200 mg/day)"** in the profile. It costs +3–8 % per day for the
example profiles.

## 4. Oils and fats
- **Rapeseed and sunflower oil are not trans fats.** Trans fats come from partial hydrogenation, and the EU has
  capped industrial trans fat at 2 g per 100 g fat since 2021. In our data, margarine has 0 g and rapeseed oil
  0.4 g trans fat per 100 g; **butter has 3.5 g** (natural ruminant trans fat). The tool now tracks trans fat and
  enforces < 1 % of energy (who2023sfa).
- **Butter is not the healthier fat.** It is 54 % saturated fat. In a network meta-analysis of 54 trials, replacing
  butter with any of safflower, sunflower, rapeseed, flaxseed, corn, olive, soybean, palm or coconut oil lowered LDL
  cholesterol by 0.23–0.42 mmol/L. Safflower and rapeseed oil ranked best for LDL (schwingshackl2018). Cutting
  saturated fat reduces cardiovascular events (hooper2020).
- **Olive oil** lowers LDL a little less than other plant oils but raises HDL a little more (ghobadi2019). Its
  strongest evidence is extra-virgin olive oil within a Mediterranean diet (estruch2018). Rapeseed oil has the
  lowest saturated fat of the common oils (6 %) and the most plant omega-3 (9 g ALA per 100 g).
- **Why oils rank high on price.** They are the cheapest calories. The tool caps them (≤ 40 g/day, fat 20–35 % of
  energy, saturated < 10 %), treats them as cooking ingredients, and the new personal ranking scores nutrients per
  100 kcal, where oils score low.
- New data in the tool: saturated, mono- and polyunsaturated, trans, n-6 and n-3 fat for 72–101 foods. Explorer
  examples: "Fat quality" and "Omega-6 ÷ omega-3".

## 5. Nutrients we don't know about
Correct, and a known limit of every diet model. Only nutrients with reference values and composition data can be
optimized. The friend's mushroom example exists: **ergothioneine**, found mostly in mushrooms, has been proposed as a
"possible new vitamin" (tian2023), but it has no requirement and no data in our databases. The hedge is variety. The
realistic plan takes every nutrient from at least 2 foods, the AI brief limits non-staples to 5 days a week, and the
"How it works" page states this limit.

## 6. Data: more foods, real foreign prices, traffic

**Larger composition databases.** Composition is not the bottleneck. Frida has about 1,300 foods, USDA SR Legacy
7,793, Fineli about 4,000 (with insoluble fibre), and CoFID (UK) about 2,900. Open Food Facts has over 3 million
products, but only with label macronutrients (no amino acids, few micronutrients). The bottlenecks for adding a food
are its **price** and its **protein digestibility**. Extending to about 200 foods is feasible: Frida for composition,
Fineli for fibre fractions, and food-group proxies for digestibility (grade C).

**Real prices for Denmark and the Netherlands.**
- **Open Prices** (Open Food Facts, ODbL) holds crowd-sourced shop receipts and labels. About 2,000 prices are
  recorded in DKK (checked 2026-10-07), so coverage is sparse but real and openly licensed. NL prices are in EUR
  mixed with other countries and need location filtering.
- **National statistics offices** (Statistics Denmark, CBS) publish price indices; whether they publish average
  prices per product still needs checking.
- **Retailer data** (e.g. Danish retailer APIs, Dutch supermarket websites) is mostly not openly licensed and needs
  permission, the same situation as Cenu Depo.
- Recommended next step: match our ~100 foods to Open Prices for DK and NL, use real prices where at least 3
  observations exist, and keep the Eurostat-level approximation as fallback with a grade per price.
- For any public use, Latvian prices must also move from Cenu Depo to an openly licensed source (CSP covers 71 of
  our foods).

**Traffic.** On Streamlit Community Cloud, the app's analytics (⋮ menu → Analytics in your workspace) show the total
number of viewers and the most recent viewers. For a private app these are the invited accounts. Only invited
people can open a private app, and sharing the link alone does not give access.

## 7. App changes in v2.2
- **Pages instead of tabs**, in three sections: Start (My profile), Improve what you eat (meal, day plan, fix my day),
  Explore the data (Explorer, Rankings, Food details, AI export, How it works). Only the open page is computed, and
  results are cached, so a click takes 0.05–0.12 s instead of 0.7 s (local). One-food day is removed.
- **Personal ranking** built from the profile (goal, price priority, targets): a weighted geometric mean of
  percentiles of nutrient score per 100 kcal, useful protein per 100 kcal, carbohydrate share, cost per 100 kcal and
  food mass per 100 kcal (direction by goal). It is paired with the **optimizer value**: the worth of the food's
  nutrients at the shadow prices of the person's optimal day ÷ its price.
- **Explorer**: formula language over every variable (now including all amino acids, total and digestible, fat
  quality, fibre types, starch), user-defined variables, filters, colour/size by formula, ranking bars,
  save/load charts.
- **Formulas shown next to every result** (rankings, meal score, day plan, fix my day, Explorer, food details), plus
  a "How it works" page.
- **Design**: owner palette (#403621, #9A6619, #9B7636, #FCD592, #CED2DE, #E7E8EB), Roboto, settings sidebar
  collapsed, a profile summary band and the country selector on every page.
